Mixed-Layer Eddies Task

Creation date: 2026/10/19

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

Summary

This design adds a planar ocean task, mixed_layer_eddies, in which a mixed-layer front spins down through mixed-layer instability. It reproduces the ice-free (c = 0) experiment of Shrestha & Manucharyan (2022) (SM22). SM22 normalize the eddy overturning by the scaling of Fox-Kemper, Ferrari & Hallberg (2008) (FFH08), so the same run tests that scaling.

The task validates Omega’s submesoscale eddy parameterization and its MPAS-Ocean counterpart (config_submesoscale_*). An eddy-resolving 500 m run is the reference. Coarse runs at 8 and 16 km run with the parameterization off and on. The task fills the testing plan in §5 of Omega’s submesoscale eddies design. Omega is the primary model; MPAS-Ocean is supported for comparison. The overturning diagnostic becomes a mesh-agnostic framework helper, which other planar tasks such as ISOMIP+ can use.

The starting point is Carolyn Begeman’s Compass draft, arctic_channel (cbegeman/compass:add_arctic_test). It has SM22’s geometry and initial condition, but this design keeps only those (see D7 under Decisions).

Success has three parts:

  • the parameterization computes what its formula says (implementation verification);

  • the resolved run reproduces SM22’s ice-free overturning (test validation);

  • the parameterized coarse runs restratify like the resolved run (parameterization validation).

The first two say whether the code and the test are correct, and each has a pass/fail check. PE-based comparisons remain report-only (D13). The combined-flux comparison reports time-averaged scaling without line fitting or fit-based pass/fail checks (D16).

Open questions

  • PE release is not a coefficient-calibration target (D15).

Requirements

Requirement: An ice-free mixed-layer front spins down in a doubly periodic domain

Date last modified: 2026/10/19

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

The task shall reproduce SM22’s ice-free configuration: a doubly periodic, flat-bottomed f-plane domain, 300 km × 200 km × 152 m, with f = 1.4 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ and a salinity-only linear equation of state. The initial state is at rest. It has a 30 m mixed layer carrying two opposing sinusoidal salinity fronts over the ITP77 Arctic profile. There is no surface forcing.

Requirement: Omega runs on its native coordinate, and MPAS-Ocean runs for comparison

Date last modified: 2026/10/19

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

Every task shall run with Omega on the p-star vertical coordinate and with MPAS-Ocean on z-star, from the same initial state.

Requirement: An eddy-resolving reference run

Date last modified: 2026/10/20

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

A 500 m run shall resolve mixed-layer instability for 15 days without the parameterization.

Requirement: Coarse runs with the parameterization off and on

Date last modified: 2026/10/20

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

Runs at 8 and 16 km, too coarse to permit mixed-layer instability, shall each run with the parameterization off and on with E3SM’s coupled defaults. Both models shall use the same parameter values.

Requirement: The eddy overturning is diagnosed consistently with the models’ discrete transports

Date last modified: 2026/10/19

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

The cross-front overturning stream function Ψ(y, z) shall be computed from edge-normal transports, the quantities the models’ continuity equations use. This applies to both the resolved eddy overturning and the parameterized one.

The computation shall be exact on any planar MPAS mesh, and other tasks shall be able to use it.

Requirement: The parameterization’s implementation is verified

Date last modified: 2026/10/19

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

A coarse run with the parameterization on shall produce the stream function that the FK11 formula gives for the run’s own state. A mismatch fails the task.

Requirement: The test reproduces the published ice-free overturning

Date last modified: 2026/10/19

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

In the resolved run, the normalized overturning |Ψ⁺| in the frontal zone, averaged over days 5–7, shall be close to 1, as in SM22. A value outside the tolerance fails the task.

Requirement: Coarse runs are compared with the resolved run

Date last modified: 2026/10/20

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

The restratification of each coarse run shall be reported relative to the resolved run. It does not fail the task.

Algorithm Design

Algorithm Design: An ice-free mixed-layer front spins down in a doubly periodic domain

Date last modified: 2026/10/20

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

The density follows SM22 (their eq. 5 and published gendata.m):

\[ \rho(y, z) = Z_S(z)\left[\Delta\rho_y Y_S(y) - \Delta\rho_z\right] + \rho_{ITP}(z), \]
\[ Z_S(z) = \tfrac{1}{2}\left[1 + \tanh\left(\frac{z + H_m}{\Delta h}\right)\right], \qquad Y_S(y) = \sin\left(\frac{2\pi (y - L_y/2)}{L_y}\right), \]

with \(H_m = 30\) m, \(\Delta h = 5\) m, \(\Delta\rho_y = 3\) kg m⁻³ and \(\Delta\rho_z = \Delta\rho_y/2\). \(L_y\) is the mesh’s periodic length, not the nominal 200 km, so the front is periodic. The front at \(y = L_y/2\) is analyzed. The other front, at \(y = 0\), closes the periodic domain.

  • EOS: \(\rho = \rho_0[1 + \beta(S - S_0)]\), with \(\rho_0 = 1027.5\) kg m⁻³, \(S_0 = 34\) and \(\beta = 10^{-3}\). Salinity is \(S = S_0 + (\rho - \rho_0)/(\rho_0 \beta)\). Temperature is constant.

  • Background profile: \(\rho_{ITP}\) comes from the ITP77 salinity profile. Above \(z = -H_m\) it is held at its value at \(z = -H_m\).

  • Noise: salinity noise, uniform in ±0.01 and drawn from a fixed seed, seeds the instability.

  • Initial flow: velocity is zero and the surface is flat. The front first undergoes Rossby adjustment, which takes about 2 days in SM22. See D5.

  • Vertical mixing: constant background diffusivity and viscosity of 10⁻⁵ m² s⁻¹, plus convective and LMD94 shear mixing. KPP is off. SM22 used KPP, but without surface forcing only its interior shear mixing acts.

  • Horizontal viscosity: constant del2 and del4, scaled with resolution. SM22 used Smagorinsky viscosity, which Omega does not have.

  • Bottom drag: quadratic, with C_d = 2 × 10⁻³ (SM22).

Rationale. The Stone (1970) fastest-growing wavelength is about 6.5 km and the e-folding time is about 9.5 h (SM22, at Ri ≈ 1). At 500 m the wavelength spans 13 cells, comparable to FFH08’s Δx = L_s/10. At 8 km it is shorter than a cell, so mixed-layer instability cannot develop on the coarse grids. At 2 and 4 km it does (D11).

Algorithm Design: Omega runs on its native coordinate, and MPAS-Ocean runs for comparison

Date last modified: 2026/10/19

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

The initial state uses the p-star initialization. The target is a 152 m geometric bottom and the surface pressure is zero. The vertical grid has 64 uniform pseudo-height levels, about 2.4 m each. As in the overflow p-star trees, MPAS-Ocean runs z-star from the converged geometric thicknesses. With a salinity-only linear EOS, pseudo and geometric thicknesses differ by under 0.5%.

Algorithm Design: An eddy-resolving reference run

Date last modified: 2026/10/20

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

The 500 m mesh has 600 × 462 cells, about 277,000 in all, which is comparable to EC30to60. A 15-day run needs about 2 × 10⁴ time steps. The run has three segments, each continuing from the restart the one before writes: a spin-up to day 5, an eddy-output segment over days 5–10 and an extension from day 10 to day 15. All write daily snapshots of the full state; the eddy and extended segments also write hourly snapshots. Spinup writes daily restarts. SM22’s overturning diagnosis still selects days 5–7. The extension shall be opt-in in both resolved and comparison tasks. Default analyses shall consume only spinup and eddy-segment output.

Algorithm Design: The eddy overturning is diagnosed consistently with the models’ discrete transports

Date last modified: 2026/10/19

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

A line \(y = y_0\) is represented by the set \(B(y_0)\) of edges whose two cells lie on opposite sides of it. Edges on a domain boundary are excluded, as are edges across a periodic seam, whose cells are more than half a period apart. Let edge \(e\) have cells \(c_1, c_2\) (cellsOnEdge). Then \(s_e = +1\) if \(y_{c_1} < y_0 \le y_{c_2}\) and \(-1\) for the reverse, because MPAS normal velocity is positive from \(c_1\) to \(c_2\). The along-front mean stream function at interface \(k\) is

\[ \Psi(y_0, z_k) = \frac{1}{L_x} \sum_{e \in B(y_0)} s_e \, d_e \sum_{k' < k} u_{e,k'} \, h_{e,k'}, \]

so \(\Psi = 0\) at the surface and \(\bar v = -\partial \Psi/\partial z\). Here \(d_e\) is dvEdge and \(h_e\) is the flux thickness on the edge. For Omega that is the pseudo-thickness, which makes the transport a mass transport. \(L_x\) is the mesh’s x_period.

The sum is the exact discrete transport from the cells south of \(y_0\) to those north of it, on any mesh. On the planar hex mesh, \(B(y_0)\) is the zigzag between two rows of cells. On other meshes it is a staircase within about one cell of \(y_0\).

Along-line means of edge quantities use the weights \(w_e = d_e |\Delta y_e| / \delta_e\), where \(\delta_e\) is dcEdge. On a Voronoi mesh the edge normal is parallel to the segment joining its cell centers, so \(w_e\) is the edge’s length projected onto x. The weights sum to \(L_x\).

  • Parameterized Ψ: take \(u_e\) as the eddy velocity (EddyVelocity in Omega, normalMLEvelocity in MPAS-Ocean). This is exactly the transport the scheme adds.

  • Resolved Ψ: use SM22’s thickness-weighted isopycnal stream function (their §2c):

    • bin each edge-layer transport \(s_e d_e u_e h_e\) by edge salinity, which measures density under this EOS;

    • average over days 5–7 and map each salinity surface to its mean depth;

    • take the eddy part as this residual overturning minus the Eulerian-mean overturning at the same depth (Abernathey et al. 2011), the integrated form of \(\overline{vh} - \bar v \bar h\).

  • Normalization:

\[ \Psi^+ = \frac{\Psi}{C_e H^2 \, \overline{\partial_y b} \, \mu(z) / |f|}, \qquad \mu(z) = \max\left\{0, \left[1 - \left(\tfrac{2z}{H} + 1\right)^2\right] \left[1 + \tfrac{5}{21}\left(\tfrac{2z}{H} + 1\right)^2\right]\right\}, \]

where \(\overline{\partial_y b}\) is the mixed-layer, x- and time-averaged buoyancy gradient. It is computed from differences of area-weighted means over y-bands about one cell wide, across the frontal zone \(|y - L_y/2| < L_y/8\) (SM22’s 75–125 km). \(C_e = 0.08\) and \(H = H_m\).

Rationale. Cell-reconstructed velocities smooth the field and do not close the discrete volume budget. Edge sums do, and need no interpolation. They also assume no mesh structure: they use only cellsOnEdge, yCell, dvEdge and dcEdge. FFH08’s Held–Schneider form, \(\overline{w'b'}/\bar b_y\), needs an Eulerian \(w\). Under z-star or p-star the models provide only velocities across moving surfaces. Binning by salinity needs only horizontal transports. See D8.

Algorithm Design: The parameterization’s implementation is verified

Date last modified: 2026/10/20

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

The parameterized Ψ on each frontal-zone edge is recovered from the eddy velocity in the last (day-15) output of each coarse on run. It is compared, edge by edge, with the FK11 formula evaluated from the same output as the models discretize it:

  • the model’s own mixed-layer depth (DenMixLayerDepth or dThreshMLD) and N² (BruntVaisalaFreqSq or BruntVaisalaFreqTop);

  • thickness-weighted means over interfaces, from the surface to the top of the first layer below the mixed-layer depth. MPAS-Ocean writes that layer’s index (indMLD). For Omega, it is found from the mixed-layer depth, measured from the sea surface;

  • each model’s conventions: H on edges is the minimum of the two cells’ in Omega and the mean in MPAS-Ocean.

Only edges whose output mixed-layer depth lies within the deepest layer with nonzero eddy velocity are compared. Elsewhere the output depth is not the one the eddy velocity used: in Omega the two come from different stages of a time step, and where the mixed layer is nearly neutral the diagnosed depth jumps by layers between them. The check fails if fewer than half the edges qualify or the normalized RMSE on them exceeds 6% at 8 km or 3.5% at 16 km. Normalize RMSE by the formula’s RMS; these thresholds allow headroom over the reported Omega errors of 5.2% and 2.9%, respectively.

Rationale. The final runs give errors of 1–5% on 92–100% of edges in Omega and about 1% on all edges in MPAS-Ocean. While the mixed layer restratifies (days 1–7), Omega’s output depth often differs from the one its eddy velocity used, so fewer edges qualify and errors reach 20% at 16 km. MPAS-Ocean clamps its mixed-layer depth to a layer midpoint in places, and its output layer depths differ slightly from those it used, so the layer index cannot be recovered from the depth; hence indMLD. Errors from a sign, a factor, a wrong frontal width or C_e of 0.06 instead of 0.08 are larger.

Algorithm Design: The test reproduces the published ice-free overturning

Date last modified: 2026/10/19

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

The check is that |Ψ⁺| at mid-mixed-layer, from the resolved run, lies in [0.5, 2]. FFH08 fit C_e = 0.06 to balanced runs and 0.08 to all runs, so the window allows for that spread and for the start from rest.

Algorithm Design: Coarse runs are compared with the resolved run

Date last modified: 2026/10/20

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

The comparison uses potential energy per unit area, \(PE = \int \rho g z \, dV / A\), and the mixed-layer mean N² in the frontal zone. These are among the measures in Omega’s design (§5). Rossby adjustment releases potential energy in every run, so the comparison uses the release after it, \(\Delta PE = PE(\text{day } 2) - PE(\text{day } 10)\), relative to the resolved run. The ratios are written to a table and plotted, with no thresholds (D13). Retain these reports and time series for inspection, not for tuning C_e (D15). Use the resolved eddy segment’s endpoint for every run’s comparison time series and final surface snapshot. Do not require the optional extended segment or include it when it happens to exist.

Algorithm Design: Time-averaged combined flux is compared with its scale

The comparison shall plot one time-averaged point for each of the five simulations against a 1:1 line. Use the single central frontal band, and form along-front covariance on native rows before averaging across the band. Sample hourly at half the diagnosed mixed-layer depth. Average signed quantities over days 6–10 using trapezoidal integration, then take magnitudes. Use the area-weighted frontal-band mean of the model’s density-threshold mixed-layer depth. Write this field in every daily and frequent forward output stream, whether the parameterization is on or off.

The abscissa is \(|\langle C_e H^2\overline{b_y}^{\,z}/|f|\rangle_t|\). The ordinate is the magnitude of the time-averaged ratio of combined flux to mean gradient at half depth. Recover the parameterized skew flux from the output eddy velocity, not from the formula being verified. Use resolved vertical velocity for the covariance, excluding ALE-total and parameterized transport velocities. Do not fit a line or apply fit-based thresholds to the combined flux (D16). MLE off runs contribute no parameterized flux. Extend the resolved eddy-output segment through day 10 and restart the extended segment there. Keep the overturning diagnostic on days 5–7.

Rationale. This adapts Figure 1 of Fox-Kemper and Ferrari (2008), Part II, to the combined transport and five individual simulations. Temporal averaging reduces snapshot variability; these points are not independent samples from the paper’s multi-simulation ensemble.

Implementation: An ice-free mixed-layer front spins down in a doubly periodic domain

Date last modified: 2026/10/20

New package polaris/tasks/ocean/mixed_layer_eddies/:

  • mixed_layer_eddies.cfg: domain, front, EOS, Coriolis ([coriolis] type = constant), vertical grid, run duration and check tolerances.

  • itp77.csv: the ITP77 profile, packaged with its provenance. It is about 150 rows at 1 dbar spacing to 153 dbar, from the Compass database file mixed_layer_restrat/itp77.csv.

The linear EOS uses eos_linear_alpha = 0, eos_linear_beta = ρ₀β, and a reference density giving ρ₀ at S₀. Omega’s linear EOS rejects a nonzero Sref.

Implementation: An eddy-resolving reference run

Date last modified: 2026/10/20

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

forward.py and forward.yaml follow baroclinic_channel, with dt_per_km and btr_dt_per_km options. One Forward class runs a segment: full for the coarse runs, and spinup, eddies and extended for the resolved run. Both models split the resolved run, because MPAS-Ocean streams cannot limit output to a time window and Polaris builds a task’s steps before it knows the model. Restarts go to a shared restarts directory, and each segment’s restart alarm is anchored at its start time, with daily restarts during spinup and a restart at the end of the eddy segment. Hourly output over days 5–10 increases storage compared with the earlier implementation, which wrote 3-hourly output over days 5–7.

Implementation: The eddy overturning is diagnosed consistently with the models’ discrete transports

Date last modified: 2026/10/19

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

A new framework module, polaris/ocean/overturning.py, works on any planar MPAS mesh, for lines of constant x or y:

  • get_line_edges(ds_mesh, coord, value) returns the edges crossing the line, with their signs and along-line weights;

  • compute_line_transport(ds_mesh, line, normal_velocity, layer_thickness) returns the signed transport per edge and layer, using the centered flux thickness that is the default in both models;

  • compute_overturning(transport) sums along the line and accumulates from the surface to give Ψ at interfaces;

  • bin_line_transport(transport, tracer, bins) returns the per-edge transport and thickness in tracer classes, for density-class overturning.

It is a dependency-light leaf module and takes arrays, not model output. The task’s diagnostics.py builds on it: it computes the salinity-binned eddy decomposition, the y-band means and Ψ⁺. It reads output through the Polaris variable mapping, so one code path serves both models.

coarse/verify.py is an analysis step in the coarse task. It evaluates the FK11 formula from each coarse run’s last output and fails the task if the relative error or the fraction of qualifying edges is outside the configured limits. Runs with the parameterization on also write N² for this check.

Date last modified: 2026/10/19

Unit tests in tests/ocean/test_overturning.py use small planar hex meshes. Lines of constant y give the row zigzag, and lines of constant x give staircases. On both kinds of line:

  • a uniform flow must give exactly its velocity times the line length;

  • a prescribed Ψ, converted to edge velocities, must be recovered to round-off;

  • the weights must sum to the line length.

Periodic and non-periodic meshes check that seam and boundary edges are excluded.

Testing and Validation: Coarse runs are compared with the resolved run

Date last modified: 2026/10/20

Contributors: Xylar Asay-Davis, Claude

Report the release ratios on the PR, with a one-off sweep of C_e (0.04 and 0.06) at 8 and 16 km in both models. None of the tasks is added to a suite. coarse is the candidate if one is ever wanted.

Decisions

D1: A separate task, not a baroclinic_channel variant

Xylar, 2026/10/19. The two share only a periodic planar mesh. The domain, initial condition, forcing and diagnostics all differ.

D2: Ice-free only; ice-covered runs are future work

Xylar, 2026/10/19. SM22’s ice runs need ice–ocean drag. Omega has no top drag, and no open PR adds it. They also need an ice-aware parameterization. For MPAS-Ocean that is E3SM#8520, which is open; Omega has none. SM22’s free-drifting ice becomes nearly immobile above c ≈ 0.68. A stationary, concentration-weighted ice lid with quadratic drag would therefore capture the regime where Ψ drops about fourfold. That is a natural follow-up once Omega has top drag.

D3: SM22’s configuration, with FFH08 through its scaling

Xylar, 2026/10/19. A single configuration keeps the cost to one resolved run. An FFH08-style mid-latitude configuration is a possible follow-up. It would have f ≈ 7.3 × 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹, a temperature front and a 50–200 m mixed layer.

D4: Doubly periodic, not a channel

Xylar, 2026/10/19. SM22 is doubly periodic. A wall-bounded channel, as in the Compass draft and FFH08, adds sidewall effects that FFH08 had to exclude from their diagnosis window. It also needs culling, and it fits a sinusoidal front poorly.

D5: Start from rest

SM22’s text says the front starts in geostrophic balance. Their published configuration, however, reads only salinity. Its initial U, V and SSH files are commented out, and they describe a 2-day adjustment. FFH08 found balanced and unbalanced starts follow the same scaling.

D6: 64 uniform levels

This replaces SM22’s 68 stretched levels, which range from 0.2 to 20 m. New Omega grids use a multiple of 16 levels. About 2.4 m spacing is close to SM22’s maximum of 2 m in the mixed layer.

D7: The Compass draft is a reference, not a port

Several parts of the draft are not carried over:

  • its hydro/nonhydro split, which needs a nonhydrostatic MPAS-Ocean fork;

  • the solitary-wave [visualize] options;

  • its stream-function scripts.

Its initial condition also has bugs, which this design avoids:

  • fVertex is zero;

  • the thermal-wind velocity is too weak by a factor of 2;

  • the SSH sign is inverted;

  • the EOS it uses does not match the model’s;

  • the sine period does not match the domain.

D8: A mesh-agnostic stream function from edge transports

Xylar, 2026/10/19. Xylar asked whether Ψ should be computed with TRiSK-consistent numerics; edge transports are those numerics.

  • Mesh-agnostic, not row sums. A first draft summed the zigzag between rows of the planar hex mesh, which would break on any other mesh. Xylar preferred a mesh-agnostic form the framework can share. It gives identical answers on hex meshes.

  • A second user. ISOMIP+’s overturning stream function, across lines of constant x, can use the same helper.

  • Alternatives rejected. Cell reconstructions and FFH08’s \(\overline{w'b'}\) form were rejected for the reasons in the diagnostic’s rationale.

D9: E3SM coupled defaults, set explicitly

Xylar, 2026/10/19. The on setting uses E3SM’s coupled values. These equal Omega’s defaults, but not MPAS-Ocean’s Registry default, whose C_e is 0.06.

D10: Full 300 km domain

Xylar, 2026/10/19. Shrinking L_x to 100 km would cut the resolved run’s cost by a factor of about 3. The full domain keeps comparisons with SM22’s figures direct. The task runs on demand.

D11: Coarse runs at 8 and 16 km

Xylar, 2026/10/20, after review by Luke Van Roekel and Carolyn Begeman. The first runs included 2 and 4 km. At 2 km the instability is resolved and at 4 km it is permitted, so the runs restratified with the parameterization off, releasing 0.89 and 0.65 times the resolved run’s potential energy. The parameterization is meant for grids that cannot permit the eddies, and 8 and 16 km are both below Δs_max.

D12: Off and on, with no FFH08-like setting

Xylar, 2026/10/20. A third setting set L_f,min = Δs_max = 2 km to hold Δs/L_f at 1. That does not ensure it: L_f is diagnosed larger once NH/|f| exceeds L_f,min. E3SM has never been run that way, so the setting was dropped and on means E3SM’s coupled defaults.

D13: The PE comparison is report-only

The PE ratios and time series do not fail a task. The original decision treated PE release as a tuning target; D15 supersedes that interpretation. The combined-flux comparison is also report-only (D16).

D14: 15-day runs

Xylar, 2026/10/20. Luke Van Roekel noted that mixed-layer eddies take about 10 days to reach finite amplitude, so 7 days is too short to compare restratification. The diagnosis window stays at days 5–7 to match SM22.

D15: PE release is not a coefficient-calibration target

The implemented closure has explicit resolution dependence. Resolved eddies and parameterized transport also develop on different schedules. Retain PE release, PE time series and stratification plots for inspection; do not infer a tuned C_e from PE ratios.

D16: Combined buoyancy flux is compared without line fitting

Use time-averaged points and a fixed 1:1 reference line, with the configured C_e. Remove the earlier provisional within-simulation slope fits and their normalized-RMSE thresholds.
